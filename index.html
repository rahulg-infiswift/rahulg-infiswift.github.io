Voice- and vision-commanded robotic pick-and-insert

Problem

An industrial robotics client wanted operators to control a robot arm by voice, in English or Japanese. Simple jogs like “move J1 10 degrees” were only the start. The real goal was autonomous tasks such as “pick up a screw and insert it into the plate”. That meant finding small, shiny, randomly oriented screws in a cluttered bin, estimating a 6-DoF grasp, planning a collision-free path, and inserting each screw into a grid of holes, reliably over long unattended runs.

Pipeline

  1. Speech to textWhisper service; Japanese is translated to English
  2. Command parsingNormalisation, entity extraction, joint-limit checks
  3. SensingPersistent dual RGB-D camera service, point clouds
  4. PerceptionRF-DETR → SAM → filtering → pick ranking
  5. Grasp pose3D stem axis → tool frame, Euler disambiguation
  6. PlanningLive scene mesh in PyBullet, IK, RRT-family planners
  7. ExecutionController SDK + gripper, retries, pose-error logging

How it works

Perception: picking from clutter

  • RF-DETR detection, then SAM instance segmentation of each candidate. GroundingDINO was evaluated and later retired.
  • A filtering cascade: ArUco-defined work area, size, confidence, stem geometry, valid depth, and rejection of false positives from shadows.
  • A depth-aware composite score ranks survivors by confidence, mask area, elongation and height, so the robot picks the top-most, clearly separated screw instead of just the most confident detection.
  • Stage-by-stage “true pick” diagnostics show where good candidates are lost.

Stem detection & grasp pose

  • PCA on the segmentation mask finds the screw's main axis. A robust local-thickness measure (95th-percentile cross-section) tells head from tail.
  • The 2D endpoints are lifted to 3D with the aligned depth map, then turned into a full tool frame (see the figure below).
  • Out of the equivalent ZYX Euler solutions, the one closest to the current pose is chosen, and the gimbal-lock case is pinned explicitly, which keeps IK stable.

3D sensing & insertion targets

  • A long-range overview camera captures scene geometry; a close-range camera is used for picking.
  • A long-lived camera process with bounded queues gives on-demand capture, frame retries, timestamped point clouds and a freshness check, so the planner never sees a stale scene.
  • The plate's hole grid comes from its CAD model and is placed in the robot frame with an ArUco tag (solvePnP).

Planning & execution

  • A PyBullet digital twin from the robot's URDF. The collision mesh is rebuilt from live point clouds (cluster selection, then Poisson, ball-pivoting or alpha-shape reconstruction).
  • IK is solved against the robot alone; obstacles are used during path planning with RRT-Connect / RRT* and smoothing.
  • A ctypes wrapper around the vendor's C controller SDK and a serial gripper driver, with timeout recovery, speed overrides for fine moves, and logging of commanded vs. actual pose.

Speech & commands

  • A FastAPI Whisper (large-v3-turbo) service. Japanese is translated to English, so one parser handles both languages.
  • Audio normalisation to 16 kHz mono with an ffmpeg fallback for phone recordings.
  • Entity extraction maps utterances to joint moves, Cartesian moves, programs, speed and tool settings, each checked against joint limits.

Operator UI & testing

  • A multi-page Streamlit app: voice input, live camera feeds, axis monitor, calibration, and a stream of the simulator.
  • pytest suites for command parsing and English/Japanese voice processing.
  • Long-duration soak tests on the physical robot.

My role

  • Task orchestrationWrote most of the main pipeline linking speech, vision, sensing, planning and execution for pick-and-place and screw insertion, including error recovery and progress reporting.
  • Vision pipeline & APIFiltering cascade, depth-aware pick scoring, PCA stem detector, 3D grasp pose estimator with the Euler and gimbal-lock fixes, and benchmarking and diagnostic tools.
  • Speech serviceBuilt the FastAPI Whisper service and audio handling. Moving to a local endpoint cut UI-to-API latency by about 2 s.
  • Camera servicePersistent camera process, on-demand capture, frame retries, point-cloud freshness checks and graceful shutdown.
  • Planning integrationRobust mesh refresh, configurable surface reconstruction, planner tuning.
  • ReliabilityTracked down long-run failures: controller timeouts, dropped camera frames, a unit mismatch in pick-point maths, and simulator rendering leaks.
  • UI & insertion targetsStreamlit operator UI and ArUco-based localisation of the hole grid.

What I learnedReal robot systems fail at the seams between perception, planning and control. Most of the engineering went into handling uncertainty: stale sensor data, controller timeouts, shadows that look like objects, and degenerate geometry like gimbal lock. That experience is why I want to study robot perception and manipulation in depth.

On-device wake-word and command detection

Problem

The voice-controlled robot needed an always-on detector on an edge device. It listens for a custom wake phrase plus short control words (start, stop), so operators don't have to press a button and a full speech recogniser doesn't have to run all the time. No recordings of the wake phrase existed, and in a noisy factory a false trigger is costly.

Pipeline

  1. Synthesise positivesQwen TTS voice cloning from VCTK and JVS speakers
  2. Mine negativesConfusable phrases, FSD50K sounds, on-site mic audio
  3. Augment & featuriseRoom impulse responses, background audio, mel + embeddings
  4. TrainSingle- and multi-class heads with balanced sampling
  5. ExportONNX, NPU-compatible layout, quantisation calibration data
  6. Stream10 ms frames on CPU or the Hailo NPU

How it works

Data generation

  • Positives are synthesised by voice cloning from reference speakers, with configurable voice variations for more speaker diversity.
  • Adaptive handling of CUDA out-of-memory errors (batch backoff and retry) keeps long generation jobs running on limited GPUs.
  • Hard negatives come from three sources: confusable speech, environmental sound categories, and real recordings from the deployment room.

Training

  • A stage-based layout (samples → dataset → features → model → eval). Every run is self-contained and traceable to its sample pool.
  • Augmentation with room impulse responses and AudioSet/FMA backgrounds, plus ~2,000 h of precomputed ACAV100M negative features.
  • Environmental negatives are sampled separately during multi-class training to keep the classes balanced.

Edge runtime

  • A streaming runtime with 10 ms frames, in synchronous and producer/consumer modes.
  • The mel frontend is reimplemented natively from pre-extracted weights, so production no longer needs the onnx package.
  • Backends for ONNX Runtime (split and merged graphs) and a Hailo NPU. I fixed the export tensor layout (B, C, H, W) so the model compiles for the accelerator.

Evaluation

  • Clip-level ground-truth scoring with a detection policy (thresholds and RMS energy gating) that matches the deployed runtime.
  • Recording of continuous audio streams to find false positives under real conditions.
  • Latency benchmarks for the mel frontend and the end-to-end runtime.

What I learnedData was the bottleneck, not the model. The design of the synthetic positives and especially the hard negatives mattered far more than changes to the architecture. Testing on continuous real-world audio exposed failures that clip-level metrics hid.
