Project A · Flagship

Vision-based industrial manipulation system

Role
Senior Data Scientist (ML & Robotics), Infiswift Technologies
Robot
6-DOF industrial arm, gripper, fixed + wrist-mounted RGB-D cameras
Tasks
Pick, place and insert, commanded by voice or UI
PyTorchGrounding DINORF-DETRSegment AnythingIntel RealSenseOpen3DPyBulletRedisWebSocketsReact

Problem

The goal was an industrial arm that finds an object with a camera, plans how to reach it, and picks, places and inserts it on its own. The objects were small, shiny screws lying at random angles, and each one had to go into a specific hole in a plate. That needed deep-learning detection, 3D perception, pose estimation, collision-free motion planning and grasping, all working together reliably over long unattended runs.

Pipeline

  1. CommandSpoken (Whisper) or from the UI
  2. SensingFixed long-range and wrist-mounted RGB-D cameras
  3. PerceptionFine-tuned detector + SAM, filtering, pick ranking
  4. Pose & grasp3D object pose → grasp angle → tool frame
  5. PlanningWorkspace mesh, IK, BiRRT / RRT*
  6. ExecutionApproach, grip, lift, move, insert, return

How it works

Detection & segmentation

  • Fine-tuned two pretrained pipelines, Grounding DINO + SAM and RF-DETR + SAM, and chose the one fast and accurate enough for live use.
  • A filtering cascade: work area defined by ArUco markers, size, confidence, stem geometry, valid depth, and rejection of false positives caused by shadows.
  • A depth-aware composite score ranks candidates by confidence, mask area, elongation and height, so the arm picks the top-most, clearly separated object.

Pose & grasp estimation

  • PCA on the segmentation mask finds the screw's main axis. A robust thickness measure tells the head from the tail.
  • The axis is lifted to 3D with depth and turned into a full grasp frame, so the grip angle follows the object's shape (see the figure below).
  • Out of the equivalent Euler-angle solutions, the one closest to the current pose is chosen, and gimbal lock is handled explicitly, which keeps IK stable.

3D perception & calibration

  • Intel RealSense and Open3D turn depth images into point clouds and a 3D map of the work area.
  • Two-camera workflow: a fixed long-range camera guides the approach, and a wrist-mounted camera refines the final insertion position.
  • The camera, work area and robot are calibrated together with ArUco markers, and points are transformed between the camera, robot and world frames.

Motion planning & grasping

  • PyBullet planning with sampling-based planners (BiRRT, RRT*). The reconstructed workspace mesh is the obstacle for collision checking.
  • Representing the workspace as one reconstructed mesh instead of many voxel bodies cut planning for the most cluttered step from about 45 s to about 3 s.
  • Inverse kinematics, plus straight-line moves for approach, grip, insert and pull-back. A failed path or IK solution is recomputed automatically instead of stopping the task.

System architecture

  • Split one large codebase into separate perception, control and interface services that communicate over Redis and WebSockets.
  • A React front end shows pipeline state, timing and failures in real time.
  • The perception and speech services keep their models loaded between tasks.

Voice commands

  • A Whisper speech-to-text service handles English and Japanese, translating Japanese to English so one parser serves both.
  • Entity extraction maps utterances to joint moves, Cartesian moves and task commands, each checked against joint limits.
  • Switching the inference backend made transcription about 3.7× faster with no loss in accuracy.

My role

  • PerceptionFine-tuned and compared the detection + segmentation pipelines. Built the filtering cascade and depth-aware pick scoring.
  • Pose & grasp3D pose and grasp-angle estimation from object shape, including the Euler-angle and gimbal-lock fixes.
  • 3D vision & calibrationRealSense + Open3D point clouds and workspace map, ArUco calibration, frame transformations, and the two-camera insertion workflow.
  • Motion planningPyBullet planning with BiRRT / RRT*, the single-mesh workspace (45 s → 3 s), IK, straight-line moves and automatic retry.

What I learnedEvery stage of this system is hand-engineered, and most of the work went into the gaps between stages: stale sensor data, shadows that look like objects, gimbal lock, planner failures. Insertion in particular showed me the limits of vision alone. That is why I want to research learned, end-to-end approaches: vision-language-action models, touch for contact-rich tasks, and learning from human demonstrations.

Project B

Spoken-command recognition pipeline

Role
Lead and sole developer
Target
Hailo edge-AI accelerator, live audio stream
Vocabulary
40+ robot command words plus background
PyTorchopenWakeWordONNXQuantisationHailo Edge AITTS data generation

Problem

Operators needed to give the robot short spoken commands, such as motion words, axes, joints, speed and tool, using an always-on model on an edge device, without a full speech recogniser in the loop. In a factory, a false trigger moves a real robot, so the model must reject near-misses and background speech. No recordings of the command vocabulary existed.

Three-stage model

  1. Mel spectrogram16 kHz audio to mel frames. Pretrained; I reimplemented it natively for the device
  2. Speech embeddingPretrained and frozen: 16 time steps × 96 features
  3. Command classifierCustom, trained by me: softmax over 40+ commands plus background

Freezing the first two stages means only a small classifier is trained. That makes training on synthetic data practical and keeps the model small enough for the edge device.

How it works

Classifier

  • A compact network of dense layers (Linear + LayerNorm blocks, configurable depth), trained with cross-entropy.
  • For the Hailo NPU, the same network was rewritten with convolutional layers, and I fixed the export tensor layout so it compiles for the accelerator.

Dataset

  • Positives synthesised with TTS voice cloning from multi-speaker English and Japanese corpora, with an automatic quality filter.
  • Easy, medium and hard negatives plus near-miss phrases, drawn from LibriSpeech speech, FMA music, FSD50K sounds and on-site microphone recordings.
  • Augmented with room impulse responses and background audio.

Edge deployment

  • Exported to ONNX, quantised with calibration data from the training set, and deployed on a Hailo accelerator.
  • A streaming runtime processes live audio continuously in 10 ms frames.

Evaluation

  • Scoring uses the same detection policy as the deployed runtime, including false positives per hour.
  • Continuous real-world audio is recorded to find false triggers that clip-level metrics miss.

Results

~300 msDetection latency, running continuously on a live audio stream.
40+ wordsRobot command vocabulary recognised by one compact classifier.
On-deviceQuantised classifier running in real time on a Hailo accelerator.

What I learnedData was the bottleneck, not the model. With the front-end stages frozen, how I designed the synthetic positives, hard negatives and near-misses mattered more than any architecture change, and testing on continuous real-world audio exposed failures that clip-level metrics hid. Building datasets carefully is a skill I expect to use directly in learning robot policies from demonstrations.
